# Standing orders

**Read this before touching anything. It is not documentation — it is the operating procedure.**
Every line below is here because a round was spent getting it wrong. When a new correction arrives, it is
added here *first*, and only then acted on, so the same mistake cannot be made twice.

> **The checked folder is `orders/`.** Start at `orders/00-READ-FIRST.md`, then run `orders/CHECKLIST.md`
> top to bottom. This page is the long form; `orders/` is the version that is actually walked every round.

Last updated: 2026-11-29 (v129 — the folder exists and is walked).

---

## 0. The report format — MANDATORY, every single delivery

Every reply that delivers work ends with these four headings, **in this order, always spelled this way**:

```
## What I did
## What you did
## What to expect
## What's next
```

- **What I did** — the changes, each with the file it lives in. No adjectives.
- **What you did** — what came from the player this round (the instruction, quoted where it matters), and what
  the player still owes the build (a screenshot, an error paste, a yes/no).
- **What to expect** — what the player will see and *where*, plus what is deliberately **not** done. If
  something is unverified, it says so here in those words.
- **What's next** — the ordered next actions, smallest first.

A delivery without all four headings is an incomplete delivery. This was missed once (v127) and the player had
to ask for it by name.

---

## 1. Never guess. Measure.

- No claim of "it works" without **either** a rendered image **or** an executed check whose output is quoted.
- When something is wrong with light, coordinates, or layout, **print the numbers first**. The v127 light bug was
  three wrong numbers plus a baked-in effect; a screenshot alone had hidden it for three rounds.
- Quote the measured delta, not the intention: *"desk +16.0/255 between tube off and on, wall +0.0"* beats *"the
  light now works"*.
- If a gate reports failure, **read what the gate is asserting before changing the art.** Three v127 "failures"
  were tests enforcing the *old* composition, not broken art.
- **Look at the render.** Every face and room fix in v127 came from looking at the picture, not the code. A
  layout that has not been looked at has not been tested.

## 2. The menu is the standard. Reuse it, do not re-draw it.

- The approved menu screen is the reference for **everything** in the game. Its materials, its props, its
  window, its monitor, its desk.
- **Reuse the generator, not the shape.** The desk's window is `make_menu_room.window(w, h)` — the same function
  that paints the menu's window, called at a larger size. Hand-painting a second window that "looks similar" is
  how v127 shipped a window the player could not recognise. Same for the monitor: `make_menu_room.crt(w, h)`.
- If a generator is not parametric yet, **make it parametric**; do not copy it.
- The menu screen is approved, therefore anything that differs from it is a bug until the player says otherwise.

## 3. Perspective and placement

- **Props stand ON the desk, and an object standing on a surface is drawn from its feet up.** In this project the
  canvas is top-down, so the bottom row of a prop is its contact point and it must sit on the desk's *far* edge
  line, standing *up* into the wall behind it. Not "resting against the near edge", not "lying flat on the wood".
  v127 put every prop's base along the near lip, which is why they read as sitting on the edge of the table.
- The desk's front edge is the bottom of the visible wood; the wood extends **up** from it toward the wall.
- **The monitor stands on the desk.** Its stand's feet meet the desk surface; its body rises above it. A monitor
  whose base is behind the desk, or whose body is hidden by the desk, is wrong however good its art is.
- Nothing important is placed in the band that another layer covers. Work out the free rows first, then author
  to them.

## 4. Layering

- `desk_layout.json` is the single source of truth for placement, and **`unity/Assets/Content/desk_layout.json`
  is the source of truth for that file** — the copy under `Resources/` is generated by
  `publish_to_unity.py` and any hand-edit there is reverted on the next publish.
- Anchors are **y-UP from the canvas bottom**. Compositing top-down needs `y = 270 - anchorY - spriteH`.
- Sort order: background -30 · monitor -20 · stand -19 · document -15 · applicant 0 · junk 8 · slab 9 · lip 10 ·
  props 13..17.
- A live light effect must **never** be baked into a sprite layer that a runtime pass is going to paint.

## 5. Palette and craft

- Every colour comes from `art/kit/palette.json`. No exceptions, no near-misses — the gate checks.
- **Hair is darker than skin. A cloth is darker than the face.** Two adjacent materials must differ in *value*,
  not just in hue. (`amber_2` and `skin_2` are the same value; that cost three drafts.)
- No ordered dither across a face. Dither is for walls and floors, where the eye averages it.
- One specular per object, on the surface that faces the light.

## 6. Engineering hygiene

- Gates that must pass, every round, all of them executed and quoted:
  `CoreTests` · `CompileCheck` · `write_unity_metas.py` · `make_sprite_manifest.py` · `publish_to_unity.py
  --verify` · `Test-ArtPipeline.ps1` · `twin_desk.py --verify` · `verify_fresh_clone.py`.
- A gate that fails to parse, or that fails for a reason unrelated to what it tests, is a **bug in the gate**.
  Fix the gate in the same commit.
- Bounds-guard every `putpixel` loop that walks a computed coordinate.
- Keep the workspace clean: no stale bundles, no scratch PNGs in the repo, no reference images in the art kit.
